If you run a small business in Cardiff and you've started asking for website quotes, you've probably noticed the prices don't line up. One designer quotes a few hundred pounds, an agency in the city quotes several thousand, and a website builder advert says you can do it yourself for a few pounds a month.

They're not necessarily wrong. They're quoting for different things. This guide explains what drives the cost of a website in 2026, what different types of site usually cost, and the questions worth asking before you commit.

What affects the price of a website?

Most of the cost of a website is time: planning, design, building, testing and getting content right. The main things that change how much time a project takes are:

  • Number of pages. A single page that explains what you do and how to contact you is a very different job from a 20-page site with a page for each service and area.
  • Content. If you supply finished text and good photos, the build is quicker. If the designer has to write copy or source images, that's extra.
  • Design approach. A lightly customised theme is cheaper than a design built around your brand from scratch.
  • Functionality. Booking systems, quote calculators, memberships and online shops all add time.
  • SEO groundwork. Page structure, titles, local wording and speed take extra care, and are what decide whether the site can be found.
  • Ongoing costs. Domain, hosting, security, updates and support continue after launch.

Typical costs for different types of website

There's no official UK price list, and published guides vary a lot depending on who's writing them. For example:

  • GoDaddy's UK guide puts the design of a basic website with a web designer at around £4,500, plus roughly £600 a year for maintenance.
  • Opace, a UK agency, models a basic 1–5 page brochure site at about £3,700–£8,600 with a freelancer and £5,700–£14,100 with a specialist agency, before VAT.
  • Bark's Cardiff web design page lists a price range of £175–£1,000 from the professionals quoting on its platform.

Those figures are describing very different services. Broadly, small businesses in Cardiff tend to see quotes fall into these groups:

  • Website builders (DIY): a monthly subscription, with your own time as the main cost.
  • Freelancers and small studios: from a few hundred pounds for a simple site to a few thousand for a larger custom build.
  • City-centre agencies: usually several thousand pounds and up, with a team of designers, developers and account managers behind the price.

One-page websites vs multi-page websites

A one-page website suits a new business, a sole trader, or anyone who needs to look credible online quickly. It can cover who you are, what you do, the areas you serve, reviews and a contact form.

A multi-page website makes sense once you offer several services or want to be found for them separately on Google. Someone searching for "boiler repair Cardiff" is more likely to find a page about boiler repair than a single page that mentions it once. More pages means more planning, writing and building, which is why the cost goes up.

WordPress vs website builders

Website builders such as Wix and Squarespace are quick to start with and cost a monthly fee. They're a reasonable choice if you want to manage everything yourself and don't expect to grow the site much.

WordPress is open-source software you own. You can move your site to any host, add almost any feature, and build out service and location pages as the business grows. The trade-off is that someone needs to keep it updated and secure, either you or your web designer.

For most small businesses that want to compete in local search, WordPress gives the most room to grow.

Why the cheapest website isn't always the cheapest option

A very cheap website can turn out expensive if it:

  • loads slowly on a phone, so people leave before it opens
  • uses a locked template you can't edit without paying again
  • has no proper titles, headings or local wording, so Google can't tell what it's for
  • sits on hosting you don't control, so leaving means starting again
  • has no one to call when something breaks

Before choosing a quote, ask what happens after launch and who owns the website.

SEO considerations for Cardiff businesses

Cardiff is a competitive place to rank. For most trades and service businesses, being found depends on:

  • a clear page for each main service
  • mentioning the areas you actually work in, such as Canton, Roath, Llanishen or Penarth, naturally rather than in a keyword list
  • a fast, mobile-friendly site
  • a complete Google Business Profile with genuine reviews
  • page titles and headings that match how people search

A good web designer builds these foundations in from the start, so you're not paying to fix them later.

What to ask before hiring a web designer

  1. Who will actually build my website?
  2. Will I own the website and the domain?
  3. Can I update the content myself?
  4. What's included: copywriting, images, SEO setup, analytics?
  5. What are the ongoing costs for hosting and maintenance?
  6. How long will it take, and what do you need from me?
  7. Can I see websites you've built for businesses like mine?

If a quote is vague on any of these, ask for it in writing.
